## <img src="https://api.iconify.design/lucide/shield-alert.svg?color=%232f81f7&width=24" width="24" height="24" alt="" /> War stories

<details>
<summary><img src="https://api.iconify.design/lucide/lock-keyhole.svg?color=%232f81f7&width=18" width="18" height="18" alt="" />&nbsp; <b>Mass certificate revocation — production never stopped</b></summary>
<br/>

**The situation.** Certificates were **revoked without warning**. Services, integrations and shop-floor equipment were all in the blast radius — and any downtime here means the production line stops. There was no time to "plan it out calmly".

**What I did:**
- Rapidly mapped the **dependency surface**: exactly where certificates were in use — Kubernetes services, ingress, service-to-service mTLS, brokers, external integrations, IoT devices
- Prioritized everything by production criticality and **coordinated several teams working in parallel**
- Issued and **rolled out new certificates**: secret and truststore updates, controlled service restarts with no traffic drop
- Verified trust chains and compatibility everywhere — including devices and legacy clients that don't speak the newer algorithms

**So it never happens again:**
- Introduced **automatic certificate rotation** and centralized secret storage
- Set up **expiry monitoring and early alerts** — instead of finding out at the moment of failure
- Wrote a **runbook** for a repeat revocation, so any on-call engineer can handle it

**Outcome:** production **kept running**, services survived the switchover, and a one-off fire turned into a managed, automated process.

</details>
